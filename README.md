### Non-Academic Reading Habits of University Students in Bangladesh

# Abstract

This study explores the non-academic reading habits of university students in Bangladesh, analyzing survey data from 138 respondents. It examines reading frequency, preferences, and obstacles, revealing a strong preference for physical books. Academic commitments and digital distractions hinder reading habits. Statistical analyses, including chi-square tests, highlight gender-based differences in reading frequency and preferences. The study also explores social media’s impact on reading habits and suggests institutional steps to promote non-academic reading. These insights are crucial for fostering a balanced reading culture in an increasingly digitalized world, encouraging students to engage more in non-academic reading.

# Methodology

The study analyzed the non-academic book reading habits of 138 students through a survey. The survey captured reading frequency, preferred book types, time spent reading, and barriers such as lack of time and digital distractions. Descriptive statistics (mean, median, mode) summarized the data, while Chi-square tests examined associations between variables like gender and book format preferences. Correlation and regression analyses explored relationships between reading time and books read annually. Data visualization tools, including charts and contingency tables, were used to present findings. The methodology ensured a robust analysis of factors influencing reading habits, providing insights to encourage non-academic reading among students.

# Introduction

This study presents a statistical analysis of non-academic book reading habits among students, focusing on frequency, preferred book types, and factors influencing reading behavior. The data reveals that a significant portion of students read non-academic books rarely, with only 23.2% reading weekly. Religious books are the most preferred, while fiction and self-help books also see considerable interest. Key barriers to reading include lack of time (37.7%) and digital distractions (32.6%). The analysis further explores correlations between reading habits, social media impact, and time spent reading, highlighting a strong positive relationship between reading time and the number of books read annually.

# Results
Frequency of Non-Academic Reading

Most students read non-academic books rarely: 31.2%, followed by weekly readers at 23.2%.

8.7% do not read any non-academic books.

Barriers to Non-Academic Reading

The most common factors discouraging reading include:

Lack of time: 37.7%

Digital distractions: 32.6%

High cost of books: 7.2%

Lack of interest: 12.3%

Other reasons: 10.1%

Preferred Book Types

Religious books: Most popular

Fiction: Second most preferred

Self-help: Also a popular choice

Books Read Annually

36.96% of students read 0-2 books annually, while 3.62% read 10-12 books.

Gender and Preferred Book Format

Chi-Square Test: Significant difference in book format preferences between genders. Females preferred physical books, while males preferred digital formats.

Time Spent on Reading

55.8% of students read for 0-2 hours per week.

Average time spent: 2.25 hours weekly.

Social Media and Reading Interest

Chi-Square Test: Significant association between social media use and interest in reading non-academic books, indicating that social media affects students' reading habits.

# Statistical Analysis

Chi-Square Tests were used to examine the relationships between variables.

Correlation Coefficient between reading time and books read: 0.75 (strong positive correlation).

Regression Analysis: Each additional hour spent on reading weekly increases the number of books read annually by 1.2 books.

# Conclusion

The study reveals that non-academic reading habits among students are limited, with a significant portion reading infrequently. However, a small group of avid readers exists. Religious books are the most popular genre, followed by fiction and self-help. Key barriers to reading include time constraints and digital distractions. The study also highlights the positive relationship between time spent reading and the number of books read annually. To foster a reading culture, it is recommended that initiatives such as book clubs, seminars, and book fairs be introduced.
